New paper Communications Physics · Oct 2026 · Open access

Self-organization and collective migration of active condensates

H. Vahid, J.-U. Sommer, A. Sharma

Cohesive droplets of attractive active particles do the opposite of what passive droplets do in a temperature gradient: they climb activity gradients. Using large-scale Brownian dynamics, we show how cohesion and spatially varying motility alone set where active condensates live, move and renew.

  • 1Condensates migrate up activity gradients, driven by outward-polarized particles at their interfaces.
  • 2Competition between activity and attraction sets their stability: they localize, split, or evaporate.
  • 3Finite clusters cycle through birth, migration and evaporation, with no biochemical feedback needed.
